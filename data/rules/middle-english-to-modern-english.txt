# Regular changes only. Modern English "father" is NOT the regular outcome of Middle English fader:
# its -th- (d > ð before -er) is sporadic and its /ɑː/ is irregular, so the rules give the regular
# /ˈfeɪdər/ and the attested form comes from data/attested.csv. Pronunciation is General American-like
# (rhotic); there is no spelling table, since Modern English spelling can't be derived by rule.

from: Middle English
to: Modern English

class C = [type=Consonant]
class V = [type=Vowel]

Loss of final -e: final unstressed ə is lost ("around Chaucer's time").
    ə → ∅ / _#

Merger of ɔːw with ɔː: the Middle English diphthong spelled ow (snow) falls together with long ɔː
(toe–tow merger), so the vowel shift gives it oʊ.
# NOTE: not on these pages; the merger is the usual account of snow, know, grow.
    ɔːw → ɔː / _C, _#

Great Vowel Shift, first phase: close-mid eː, oː rise to iː, uː, and the close vowels iː, uː become
diphthongs.
# NOTE: the page says uː did not shift before labial consonants or after j (soup); not modelled.
    iː uː eː oː → ei ou iː uː

Great Vowel Shift, second phase: aː rises to æː (around 1550); after 1600 æː rises to ɛː, and the open-mid
ɛː, ɔː rise to eː, oː.
    aː → æː
    æː ɛː ɔː → ɛː eː oː

Later developments: the new diphthongs become aɪ, aʊ; the meat vowel (eː, from ME ɛː) merges with the meet
vowel iː; the mate vowel (ɛː, from ME aː) becomes eɪ; the boat vowel oː becomes oʊ.
# NOTE: "great", which kept /eɪ/, is one of the exceptions the page mentions.
    ei ou → aɪ aʊ
    eː ɛː oː → iː eɪ oʊ
