# Proto-Germanic → West Germanic
#
# Sources (retrieved 2026-11-07):
#   Wikipedia, "West Germanic languages", sections "Origins and characteristics" and "Phonology"
#     https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germanic_languages
#   Wikipedia, "Phonological history of Old English", section "Unstressed vowel reduction", steps 1–2,
#     which it dates to West Germanic times
#     https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonological_history_of_Old_English
#
# The West Germanic page gives the consonant changes in a partial order ("must have occurred after");
# it doesn't order the vowel changes, so their order here is a choice (NOTE: lines).

from: Proto-Germanic
to: West Germanic

class C = [type=Consonant]
class V = [type=Vowel]
class N = {m, n}

# ---- Vowels ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A-mutation: u is lowered to o when ɑ or ō is in the next syllable.
# NOTE: the page gives only the trigger. The usual exceptions (no a-mutation before a nasal plus
# consonant, as in *hundą > hund, or when i or j intervenes) aren't on the page; both are included
# (*brugjō has y in bryċġ, not o). Placed first, since the vowels that trigger it change below.
    u → o / _C(C){ɑ, ɑ̃, ɔː, oː} // _NC, _j, _Cj

Raising of ō: Proto-Germanic ɔː (ō) is raised to oː, at every length and nasal or not.
    ɔː ɔ̂ ɔ̃ː ɔ̃̂ → oː ô õː õ̂

Final ō: absolutely final non-nasal ō is raised and shortened to u; other final-syllable ō becomes ā.
# From the Old English page, which says overlong ô and ō in medial syllables were unaffected.
    oː[-stressed] → u / _#
    oː[-stressed] → ɑː / _C(C)#

Overlong vowels: overlong vowels become simple long vowels.
    V[+overlong] → V[-overlong]

Lowering of ē¹: Proto-Germanic ē (ɛː) becomes ā, or ō before a nasal.
# NOTE: stressed syllables only. Unstressed ē is left for the Old English shortening of unstressed long
# vowels, which gives *fadēr > fæder; lowering it too would give *fædar.
# NOTE: ē¹ and the rare ē² aren't distinguished in this data, so all stressed ē is treated as ē¹.
    eː[+stressed] → oː / _N
    eː[+stressed] → ɑː

Unstressed ai: unstressed ai becomes ē.
    ɑ[-stressed]i → eː

# ---- Consonants (in the page's order) -----------------------------------------------------------

zw and ðw: zw and ðw become ww.
    zw ðw → ww ww

Fortition of ð: ð becomes d in all positions (after zw, ðw > ww).
    ð → d

Delabialisation: labiovelars lose their labialisation except word-initially.
# NOTE: Ringe & Taylor (cited by the page) treat them as kw-type clusters first; here they simply lose
# rounding.
    kʷ xʷ ɣʷ → k x ɣ // #_

Loss of final z: word-final z is lost.
    z → ∅ / _#

Rhotacism: the remaining z merges with r.
    z → r

# Loss of final -a (including -ą) in polysyllables is listed here by the West Germanic page, but the
# Old English page's worked examples (*faraną > faran vs. *faranaz > faren) need those vowels to
# survive Old English a-fronting and a-restoration. It's applied in the Old English file instead.

Final j: j left at the end of a word after a consonant becomes i.
# From the Old English page ("Loss of -(i)j-"): when -j- became word-final after loss of a following
# vowel, it was converted into -i.
# NOTE: here it fires only once the following vowel has gone, which in this data happens in Old English;
# the rule is repeated there.
    j → i / C_#

West Germanic gemination: every consonant except r is doubled before j.
    p t k ɸ θ x s β d ɣ m n l → pp tt kk ɸɸ θθ xx ss ββ dd ɣɣ mm nn ll / _j

Allophones of b: b is a stop word-initially, after m and when doubled; β only elsewhere.
# From the page's Phonology section ("[β] was an allophone of /b/ word medially, except when preceded
# by /m/ or geminated"). Grimm's law wrote every b as β, so the stop is restored here.
    β → b / #_, m_
    ββ → bb
